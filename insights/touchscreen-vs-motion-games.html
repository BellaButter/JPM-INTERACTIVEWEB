TOUCHSCREENS & KIOSKS · PRACTICAL GUIDES

Touchscreen games or motion-sensing games: which suits your booth?

Touchscreens and motion sensors are the two approaches we use most in booth games. Touchscreens are easy to play and need little space, while motion games draw more onlookers. Choose by what you want people to do, not by which technology looks more advanced.

JPM Media Space·Published 11 November 2026·3 min read

A side-by-side comparison

PointTouchscreenMotion sensing
Best forGames where people choose answers, read information, or tap targetsGames where people run, jump, or wave
Floor spaceWorks in a small boothNeeds room to stand and play in front of the screen
OnlookersPeople see it up closeVisible from far away, and a scene people want to photograph
What to checkScreen height, reach, and button sizeLighting, sensor mounting distance, and passers-by who interfere
ScreensYour touchscreen or the organiser’sAn LED screen or projector, with a sensor such as LiDAR or a depth camera

Touchscreens: think about reach

A big screen does not mean every point is within reach. We place the buttons people use most at a height most people can touch comfortably, including children and wheelchair users, and we test repeated taps on the real screen before show day. Try Tap the Right Item to feel how a touch game plays.

Motion sensing: think about space and light

You need a clearly marked play area, separate from the walkway and the queue, so passers-by do not confuse the game. We choose the sensor and where to mount it from the real booth layout, and fine-tune it on site before the event opens. An example from our work is LAZADA Run Thailand, a running game where people move in front of the screen (portfolio with employer).

You do not have to choose just one

Some booths use a motion game to draw people in, then a touchscreen at another spot for sign-up or browsing products. People who would rather not move around can still take part. Tell us your booth size and the screens you have, and we will help you plan it. See how we build booth games, or read our guide to event booth games.

FAQ

What sensors do motion games use?

It depends on the game and the space, for example LiDAR or a depth camera. We choose once we have seen the booth layout.

Can a small booth use a motion game?

Yes, if there is enough room to stand and play. Send us your booth layout to check first.

Can we use the venue’s screen?

Yes. Tell us its size, resolution, and touch system, and we will check and test it before show day.

What is an interactive wall (experience wall), and what kind of event suits one?

An interactive wall is a large screen or a projection on a wall that responds when people walk past, stand in front of it, or raise a hand. The image moves with them straight away, so people walking past stop to look, and people who stop want to take a photo home. It suits booths that want to stand out in the hall, and permanent spaces such as lobbies and showrooms.
