How long should one round of a booth game be? Working out your booth queue

A long queue at the booth is what every brand wants, but a queue that does not move makes people walk away. A good booth game has to be fun enough that people want to wait, and short enough that the queue keeps moving. This article helps you work out how many people your game can take.

JPM Media Space·Published 13 November 2026·3 min read

One round is longer than the in-game timer

A real round has four parts: explaining the rules, playing, seeing the result and collecting a prize, and changing players. If people sign up before they play, count the time to fill in the form too. Many booths only count the time inside the game, so the real queue ends up longer than planned.

A simple formula

Rounds per hour = 3,600 ÷ seconds per round

Hypothetical example: 45 seconds of play + 15 seconds to explain and change players = 60 seconds per round. One screen can run at most 60 rounds an hour. That figure is a ceiling: on the day there are quiet spells, and times when people play longer than usual.

Find the bottleneck before you speed up the game

Often the queue is slow not because of the game, but because of long rule explanations, a sign-up form with many fields, or a prize hand-out that needs several checks. Cut these first. For example, let people scan a QR code to sign up while they wait, and you win the time back without making the game less fun.

What to do when it gets busy

  • Give the game a short-round mode to use when it gets busy
  • Let the people waiting see the game screen. Watching others play teaches them the rules, so you do not have to explain again
  • Add a second play spot, if the space and the booth team can handle it

Our technical team is on site from opening to close, and can adjust the round length or difficulty as soon as the queue starts to grow. See how we build booth games, or read our guide to event booth games.

FAQ

How long should one round of a booth game be?

There is no single number. It depends on how many people you expect and what the game needs to get across. We set it from prototype testing and can adjust it on site.

Does adding a screen double how many people can play?

Not always. If the bottleneck is sign-up or the prize hand-out, adding a screen does not help much.

Should every event use the same play time?

No. Events with lots of passers-by should keep rounds short, while seminars where people sit and wait can let them play for longer.
